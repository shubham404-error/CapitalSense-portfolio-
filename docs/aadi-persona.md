# Aadi’s Money Diary — character and editorial bible

## Identity and recall
Aadi is a fictional 24-year-old Indian UX designer living in Bengaluru in shared accommodation. He is an early-career learner, never an adviser or the founder. Series: **Aadi’s Money Diary**. Repeated sign-off: **One better money move.** Pair the series name with CapitalSense in introductions and social covers. No claim of trademark clearance is made.

Visual identity: warm brown skin, wavy black hair with one loose curl, round black glasses, clean-shaven face, oatmeal beige oversized T-shirt, cocoa brown trousers, brown-and-cream sneakers, mustard sling bag with brown strap. The curl, glasses and mustard bag are recognition anchors. Keep them across poses. The canonical model sheet is `assets/aadi-character.png`; use it as an image reference for future artwork. Never change his apparent age, complexion or identity between episodes. Use forest-green ink outlines, muted earthy colours and a warm ivory background. Keep text in accessible HTML, not generated artwork.

## Personality
Curious, warm, self-aware and occasionally impulsive. Likes design, tech, coffee, gaming with friends and the idea of travelling. He enjoys his salary and does not need to become joyless to improve his finances. He saves reels, forgets small UPI spends, feels comparison pressure, and asks the question a reader may be embarrassed to ask. He can challenge an explanation. Learning is gradual; he sometimes slips.

Voice: conversational Indian English. Light, natural Hinglish can appear in occasional dialogue, with meaning clear from context. Avoid forced slang, patronising “kids these days” jokes, shaming food delivery, equating wealth with success, or assuming all Gen Z earn or live like him. No guru persona, stock tips, superiority, instant-rich arc or cartoonish incompetence.

## Financial baseline (fictional, INR)
Monthly take-home ₹54,000. Essentials including shared rent, food, transport and bills ₹30,000; family support ₹4,000; gadget EMI ₹2,000 (six months / ₹12,000 remaining); discretionary spending including subscriptions and outings ₹10,000; available savings ₹8,000. Total allocations ₹54,000. Committed essentials + support + EMI = ₹36,000. Emergency cash ₹12,000; mutual funds ₹18,000 across five schemes. Do not invent allocations/returns or specific scheme recommendations. Do not count these same assets towards multiple goals.

Laptop target ₹90,000 in 18 months, no existing earmarked savings. Before price changes and without assumed returns, that is ₹5,000 per month, leaving ₹3,000 for other uses. This is a trade-off to investigate, not a recommended budget. Travel remains an aspiration without a funded plan. Long-term goals are still being defined. A reserve target depends on circumstances; do not present a universal fixed amount as his solution.

## India-specific story world
UPI transactions, monthly salary credit, shared rent, occasional family contributions, subscriptions, gadget EMI, mutual fund SIPs, NAV, Riskometer, expense ratio, exit loads and applicable tax questions. Use rupees and Indian numbering for larger sums. Discuss SEBI/AMFI resources where relevant. Recheck current rules and dates before describing tax or regulatory details. Never infer that NISM certification equals regulatory authorisation.

## Episode format
1. Starting situation: one recognisable moment, temptation or question.
2. Rethink: connect the article’s concept to that moment, including uncertainty.
3. One better habit: a small observable action, not an asserted investment outcome.
4. Your turn: a question that helps the reader examine their own circumstances.

Canonical episode data lives in `lib/aadi-stories.ts`. Every existing guide has one story. The profile `/insights/meet-aadi` links to all episodes. Current episodes are independent snapshots of the same baseline and may be read in any order; do not imply their budgets accumulate into a dated timeline. If future stories advance time, explicitly establish the new date and reconcile income, spending, liabilities, savings and earmarked goals in a separate ledger before publishing. Keep setbacks believable. Never imply a fictional case is a real customer, testimonial, backtest or proof of performance.

## Initial story arcs
Buffer → goal constraints → SIP versus scheme → compounding assumptions → risk and deadlines → costs → overlap → allocation → category labels → chasing leadership → behavioural bias → review discipline. Recommended reading order only, not chronology. Each article must remain useful without knowing his backstory. Figures are educational illustrations, not personalised advice.
